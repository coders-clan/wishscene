# wishscene voice and copy

Version 0.1 · Use with [brand strategy](BRAND_STRATEGY.md)

## Voice rules

Write like a creative partner giving clear directions. Short, specific, inviting. Let the user imagine; explain every consequential action and cost. Do not claim something happened in real life. Avoid “fake,” “fool,” “proof,” “undetectable,” or claims of guaranteed realism.

**Do:** “Build a scene in Kyoto at dusk.” “This photo looks different from your references—try another angle.” “Changing the jacket will regenerate two scenes.”

**Don't:** “Convince everyone you went to Kyoto.” “Perfectly real in seconds.” “Your memories, created.”

## Tone by moment

| Moment | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Wonder plus clarity | “Picture yourself somewhere new.” |
| Onboarding | Reassuring and direct | “Add a few photos so the scenes look like you.” |
| Planning | Collaborative | “Let’s choose four moments for this story.” |
| Waiting | Honest | “Creating photo candidates. You can leave this page.” |
| Review | Respectful | “Only you can decide if this looks like you.” |
| Error | Specific and useful | “We couldn’t use this photo because two faces are visible. Choose one with only you.” |
| Privacy | Plain and precise | “Delete your profile and its generated images in Settings.” |

## Core copy

Hero headline: **Imagine it. Make the scene.**

Hero body: “Turn a place or moment you imagine into a connected story of photos, short videos and posts starring you.”

Primary CTA: “Create a scene.” Secondary CTA: “Explore an example.”

Three-step explainer:
1. “Add photos of yourself.”
2. “Direct your experience.”
3. “Choose your favorite scenes and make a story.”

The third step intentionally includes user approval. Do not suggest that a publish-ready pack is automatically correct.

## Error and empty states

No experiences: “Your next scene starts with an idea. Choose a place, a moment, or a mood.”
Too few references: “Add more angles so we can keep your face consistent.”
Low likeness: “This one may not look like you. Try another reference or generate new options.”
Provider failure: “This scene didn’t finish. Your other scenes are safe. Try again.”
No approved images: “Approve at least one scene to start your story pack.”
Budget threshold: “This video may use more credits than your remaining balance. Review the estimate before starting.”

## Social caption drafts

Caption drafts should be creative or explicitly hypothetical. Examples:
- “A little daydream in full color.”
- “If I could step into one scene today, it might look like this.”
- “Somewhere between imagination and a postcard.”

For an AI-created image, never auto-generate “Just landed,” “Finally made it,” or a real-world claim of attendance. User can edit text, but export UI keeps a clear AI-created disclosure and provenance.

## Style details

Use second person in guidance and first person only for user-controlled actions (“My scenes”). Use “photo,” “clip,” “story,” and “experience” in UI; use “asset,” “provider,” and “Story Bible” only in engineering docs. No emoji in errors or privacy notices. Avoid unnecessary exclamation marks. Localize meaning, not literal idioms, for Hebrew RTL.

## Translation notes

Proposed Hebrew headline for testing: **מדמיינים רגע. יוצרים ממנו סיפור.** This is a concept translation, not approved final copy. Test with Hebrew speakers for naturalness and whether “scene” evokes theatre rather than an imagined moment. Keep brand name in Latin lowercase until a Hebrew treatment is intentionally designed.
